Basal melt rates under Antarctic ice shelves peak near the grounding zone, where
warm, salty seawater intrudes into the channel beneath the ice. Ocean-cavity
models work with kilometre-scale grid cells and cannot resolve that intrusion, so
they fall back on melt-rate parameterizations, while the models built specifically
for the intrusion are one- or two-dimensional and assume up front whether the flow
is laminar or turbulent. Together with collaborators I simulate the grounding-zone
channel in three dimensions with a melting boundary condition, determining the
flow regime from the simulations instead of prescribing it, and systematically
varying the meltwater inflow rate and the channel height.
